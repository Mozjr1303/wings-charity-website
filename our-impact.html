SAVING LIVES IN MALAWI

Advancing SDG 3: Tackling Maternal & Newborn Mortality

Malawi faces some of the highest maternal and neonatal mortality rates in the world. Wings directly bridges the fatal gap between hospital discharge and community survival by delivering intensive in-home care for the critical first 0 to 3 months.

TARGETED INTERVENTION

The Two Root Causes Wings Directly Addresses

Malawi’s severe rates of maternal and neonatal loss stem from complex factors. Wings concentrates its full clinical and humanitarian resources on two decisive drivers:

ROOT CAUSE 01

Poverty & Inequality

Widespread poverty, acute food insecurity, and limited educational access exacerbate severe health complications. When a mother suffers postpartum haemorrhage, eclampsia, or loses lactation capacity, an impoverished household cannot afford infant formula, clean bottled water, electricity, or dedicated childcare.

Wings Response: We remove the financial barrier completely by providing free infant formula, warm clothing, private nannies, and paying electricity and water bills for 0–3 months.
ROOT CAUSE 02

Inadequate Prenatal & Postnatal Care

Insufficient antenatal visits, delays in reaching skilled delivery facilities, and the near-total absence of structured postnatal follow-up contribute to fatal complications. When discharged home without monitoring, babies of incapacitated mothers quickly succumb to hypothermia, malnutrition, or untreated infection.

Wings Response: We collaborate with government hospital maternity teams for clinical case vetting and place trained private nannies directly inside the home for daily vigilance.
UN SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT GOAL 3

Aligning with SDG 3: Good Health & Well-Being

United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 3 aims to ensure healthy lives and promote well-being for all at all ages. Specifically, Malawi is working towards ambitious targets:

  • Halving Maternal & Newborn Deaths: Drastically reducing preventable neonatal mortality and stillbirths through targeted post-delivery intervention.
  • Improving Quality of Care: Strengthening emergency care pathways, hospital discharge safety, and quality-improving interventions in health facilities.
  • Protecting Vulnerable Kinship Caregivers: Providing economic and care relief so grandmothers and extended families are never forced into catastrophic debt or child neglect.
"We don’t support all babies. Only cases where mum cannot care for the child. By focusing where the clinical and economic crisis is most acute, we prevent newborn deaths that would otherwise slip through the cracks of a burdened system."
— Wings International Women-Led Initiative
