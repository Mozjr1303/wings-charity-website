FIELD PERSPECTIVES & CASE STUDIES

Stories of Impact in Maternal & Newborn Care

Behind every maternal health programme is a mother, a newborn, and an extended family overcoming crisis. Explore our in-depth programme case study profiles explaining how Wings supports families across three core scenarios in Malawi.

Ethical Storytelling & Safeguarding Notice: To uphold the dignity, privacy, and safeguarding of the families we serve, Wings never fabricates beneficiary identities, personal histories, or medical claims. The three in-depth profiles below explain the real clinical and community care pathways our programmes address in Malawi and serve as structured templates ready for consent-approved beneficiary case studies.
Neonatal Care & Safeguarding Case Study Template 01

Protecting High-Risk Newborns in the First Weeks of Life

The Maternal & Neonatal Challenge

When babies in Malawi are born prematurely, with low birth weight, or as twins and triplets, the transition from a hospital neonatal unit back to a rural or peri-urban home carries significant risk. Without reliable warmth, clean cord care, and frequent weight monitoring, fragile newborns are highly susceptible to hypothermia and neonatal infections during their first 28 days.

How Wings Responds

Through our Protection & Safety and First 100 Days programmes, Wings equips mothers with Essential Newborn Packages—including knitted hats, warm blankets, clean wraps, and hygiene supplies—while reinforcing Kangaroo Mother Care (continuous skin-to-skin contact) and scheduling close postnatal nursing follow-ups.

Postnatal Maternal Health Case Study Template 02

Accompanying Mothers Through Postpartum Recovery

The Maternal & Neonatal Challenge

Many women in Malawi travel long distances from rural villages to reach referral hospitals when labor becomes obstructed or complicated. Following emergency caesarean sections, heavy blood loss, or severe pre-eclampsia, mothers are often discharged home while still physically weak, anemic, and vulnerable to post-surgical infection.

How Wings Responds

Our women-led Compassionate Care programme accompanies recovering mothers after hospital discharge. We support wound-care hygiene, monitor maternal recovery indicators, assist mothers in keeping up with their Malawi Woman Health Passport appointments, and provide empathetic guidance so both mother and infant heal safely.

Family & Community Support Case Study Template 03

Standing Beside Grandmothers and Extended Caregivers

The Maternal & Neonatal Challenge

When a mother experiences critical postpartum illness or tragically dies during childbirth, grandmothers and aunts immediately step in to care for the newborn. Despite deep love and dedication, extended families in low-resource communities often struggle to afford safe infant nutrition, hygiene supplies, and transport to postnatal clinics.

How Wings Responds

Our Family Support programme walks alongside grandmothers and kinship caregivers during the critical first 100 and 1,000 days. By providing practical infant care bundles, safe feeding guidance, and regular health-worker check-ins, we help ensure vulnerable infants survive and thrive within the warmth of their own extended families.

SAFEGUARDING & DIGNITY

Our Ethical Storytelling Standards

Every mother and child supported by Wings has the right to privacy, dignity, and accurate representation. We follow four strict standards whenever field stories are documented.

Enquire About Field Reports
Standard 01

Informed Consent First

No personal story or photograph is published without clear, voluntary, and informed consent in the family's own language.

Standard 02

Care Never Conditional

Access to maternal care, Essential Packages, and family support is unconditional and never tied to sharing a personal testimony.

Standard 03

Verified Clinical Accuracy

All programme outcomes and health descriptions are reviewed for clinical accuracy without exaggeration or unverified claims.

Standard 04

Respectful Portrayal

We highlight the strength, love, and resilience of Malawian mothers, grandmothers, and nurses alongside the challenges they overcome.
