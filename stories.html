FIELD CASE SCENARIOS & CARE PATHWAYS

Stories of Survival: When Mothers Cannot Care

We don’t support all babies. We step in when catastrophic obstetric complications strike—ensuring that babies whose mothers face coma, severe illness, eclampsia, or lack mental capacity receive round-the-clock care, formula, nannies, and paid utilities during their first 0–3 months.

Ethical Storytelling & Safeguarding:

To uphold the dignity, privacy, and clinical confidentiality of the mothers and families we serve in Malawi, Wings never publishes sensationalized photos or identifiable personal data without full informed consent. The three case profiles below reflect real clinical referral pathways vetted in partnership with government hospital maternity teams.

Hospital Referral Scenario 01 Emergency Clinical Referral

Postpartum Haemorrhage (PPH) & Maternal Coma

The Clinical Emergency

Following an emergency caesarean section at a district hospital in Malawi, a 22-year-old first-time mother suffered catastrophic postpartum haemorrhage (PPH), resulting in hypovolemic shock, multiple blood transfusions, and prolonged coma in intensive care. With the mother unconscious and fighting for life, her newborn girl was left without breastmilk or immediate maternal care.

The Wings Care Lifeline

Hospital ward nurses immediately referred the case to Wings under our strict clinical criteria. Wings enrolled the infant into our comprehensive 0–3 month care programme:

  • Dedicated Private Nanny: Placed directly in the family home to take charge of nocturnal feedings, hygiene, and infant safety.
  • Full Formula & Essentials Kit: Safe infant formula supplied consistently, alongside sterilising kits, warm clothes, and blankets.
  • Paid Electricity & Water Utilities: Direct funding of household power and water bills so formula bottles could be safely sterilised.

Outcome: By month two, the mother emerged from coma and was discharged home to find a healthy, thriving infant in clean, supported surroundings.

Hospital Referral Scenario 02 Severe Obstetric Trauma

Eclampsia Complications & Acute Lactation Failure

The Clinical Emergency

Severe pre-eclampsia progressed into full eclamptic seizures during labor for a young mother living in severe rural poverty. While both mother and baby survived delivery, the mother’s neurological trauma, severe hypertension medications, and physical exhaustion caused complete, irreversible lactation failure. With zero household savings, purchasing commercial formula was impossible.

The Wings Care Lifeline

The government hospital obstetric team verified the mother's inability to nurse and the extreme household poverty. Wings stepped in with immediate zero-cost support:

  • 100% Free Infant Formula: High-quality infant formula supplied on an unbroken schedule for the full 0–3 month duration.
  • Private In-Home Nanny: Assisting the mother during dizzy spells and seizures, ensuring the baby was never dropped or left unattended.
  • Domestic Utility Support: Guaranteed running water and electric power for reliable boiling and warm swaddling.

Outcome: The newborn gained weight steadily on WHO growth charts, while the mother received uninterrupted rest to stabilize her blood pressure.

Hospital Referral Scenario 03 Psychiatric & Kinship Support

Maternal Mental Incapacity & Kinship Caregiver Backing

The Clinical Emergency

Following an acutely traumatic labor, a mother suffered severe postpartum psychosis and complete lack of mental capacity to care for or recognize her newborn baby. To protect the infant from accidental harm or neglect, the baby was discharged into the care of an elderly grandmother who survived on seasonal subsistence farming and had no money for milk or baby supplies.

The Wings Care Lifeline

Vetted and approved by hospital clinical social workers, Wings wrapped a full protective circle around the grandmother and child:

  • Dedicated Private Nanny: Working side-by-side with the grandmother to handle nighttime feedings, swaddling, and clinical clinic checkups.
  • Complete Baby Nutrition & Clothing: Free infant formula, feeding bottles, sanitizers, and weather-appropriate baby clothes for 0–3 months.
  • Utility Bills Covered: Clean water and home electricity funded 100% free of charge to relieve catastrophic household financial strain.

Outcome: The baby remained safe, loved, and properly nourished within family kinship while the mother underwent psychiatric outpatient care.
